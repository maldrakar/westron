# 0003. The look of 2026-11-20

Date: 2026-11-20. Status: the owner's choice of the look, made on prototype renders; the contract and the snapshot
matrix that implement it wait for the owner's review together (decisions/0005, the second gate), as the owner asked.

## Context

At the second gate of phase 3 the owner found Button's function and colors right and its look not yet (#85). The
owner compared three prototype looks, each with a page of sizes and a page of states, with Apple's own button styles,
on an iPhone 17 in light (catalog renders of a throwaway prototype that changed no token, contract or WestronUI code):

- A, quiet: a capsule; secondary filled with `color.background.muted`, no border; 32, 44 and 52 tall; the icon at
  the symbol's medium scale on the label's baseline.
- B, tinted: a capsule; secondary filled with `color.background.accent-subtle` and an accent label; 28, 36 and 50
  tall, padding 14, 18 and 22; the icon at the small scale on the label's baseline, gap 4, 4 and 6.
- C, outlined: a capsule; secondary outlined in `color.border.default`; 32, 40 and 48 tall; the icon centered.

The public API stays: `.buttonStyle(_:size:iconOnly:)`, the variants `primary`, `secondary` and `tertiary`, the sizes
`s`, `m` and `l`, the role, the states, behavior and accessibility as the contract states them.

## The owner's decisions

1. **Shape: a capsule** for every variant and size; icon-only is a circle whose diameter is the height. The platform's
   capsule, never a large radius (design-language/04). Flat, opaque fills in westron's colors, no glass and no
   material. `radius.control` stays a token for the fields and the segmented control; the fields are redesigned
   separately.
2. **Secondary: A's neutral fill instead of the outline.** At rest `color.background.muted`, the label, the icon and
   the progress indicator in `color.text.primary`, no border. Hover and pressed darker steps of the same fill; disabled
   and loading keep the resting fill, as primary does. With the destructive role: the same fill and the same hover and
   pressed, the words in `color.status.danger.text`.
3. **Primary and tertiary keep their colors.** Tertiary still takes `background.hover` and `pressed` under the pointer
   and the finger, now in the capsule.
4. **B's heights on iOS and iPadOS: 28, 36 and 50** for `s`, `m` and `l`, in the shared `size.control.*`, so for every
   control. macOS keeps 24, 32 and 40. The owner did not see watchOS, which keeps 32, 44 and 52 for now. No hit area
   may shrink on any platform.
5. **Padding 16, 20 and 24** (`space.200`, `space.250`, `space.300`), the same on both sides, on every platform.
6. **The label's font**: `footnote.strong`, `subheadline.strong` and `body.strong`; `l` was `subheadline.strong`.
7. **B's icon seating.** Beside the label, the symbol's small scale in the label's font, on the label's first text
   baseline, not centered by its frame; gap 4, 4 and 6 (`space.50`, `space.50`, `space.75`). The progress indicator
   still takes the icon's place without changing the width.
8. **Unchanged:** the press scale of 98 %, the motion tokens, disabled at `opacity.disabled`, the loading rules, the
   focus ring.

## Decided by the implementing agent

Under the owner's instruction to implement the look, these are the agent's, for the owner to confirm at the review:

- **The muted fill's hover and pressed tokens** (decisions/0012, revision of 2026-11-20): solved by the palette, as
  far from `muted` as every word still reads, which is step 5's tone. See "Open", 1.
- **The high-contrast outline.** design-language/02 asks that the edge of a control a person must find meet the
  non-text target, and the high-contrast themes keep the canvas and steps 1–5, so the muted fill stays about 1.1:1 off
  the canvas there. The secondary button keeps its outline in the high-contrast themes only, in every state,
  `border.strong` or `status.danger.border` at `stroke.thin`, drawn over the fill. A case has no contrast key, so the
  contract's description states it, as Surface's does for its line.
- **`size.target.minimum`.** A platform token for the least side of a hit area, 44 on iOS, iPadOS and watchOS and 28
  on macOS, so that a control's height and its hit area are separate. Button's contract binds its hit area statement to
  it; the text field's hit area takes it, which removes the raw 28 its code carried for macOS; the check keeps the
  larger of it and `size.control.m`, so its macOS hit area stays 32. The catalog's hit-area tests measure that none
  shrank (#85's report).
- **`capsule` in the schema.** A part's `shape` binds a shape instead of a token, `capsule`, and nothing else binds
  one (`spec/schema/component.schema.json`, `tools/westron spec check`). The icon's small scale and its baseline are
  in the description and the Apple notes: the symbol scale is Apple's, and the alignment is layout, not a token.
- **Continuous ends.** SwiftUI's `Capsule()`, whose ends are continuous by default, as the prototype the owner chose
  drew it, and as design-language/04 asks of corners on Apple. In a square, icon-only's frame, it draws a circle (the
  snapshot matrix). Where a label at a large text size makes the capsule tall, the continuous ends read a little
  flatter than half circles; the accessibility5 sheets show it.
- **Icon-only** draws the glyph at the same small scale as beside a label, as prototype B did: next to B's render
  the catalog's circles look the same, and a medium glyph would make icon-only heavier than the label beside it.
- **The progress indicator is scaled to the icon's slot.** At the small scale the icon is smaller than the system's
  smallest spinner at size s (on macOS 9 pt against 10, measured by ButtonProgressTests), and a frame does not shrink a
  spinner, so the indicator draws it scaled down to fit; the width still does not change. Without an icon it takes an
  icon's slot on every OS now, as it did on macOS.
- **The catalog wraps.** The states pages wrap their rows, and the behavior page's buttons and toggle take at most the
  page's width, so a label wraps between words: with the wider padding, three destructive buttons of size s no
  longer fit beside the state names on a phone.

## Open

1. **How dark the secondary button gets when pressed.** The destructive secondary writes `status.danger.text` on the
   pressed fill, and that red, like every colored word, is solved to just reach 4.5:1 (7:1 in high contrast) on step
   5. So the pressed fill can darken only to step 5's tone: in light `#EBEEF4` → `#E8ECF1` → `#E5E9EE`, about 1.11,
   1.13 and 1.16 against the canvas; in dark `#22272D` → `#262A31` → `#2A2E35`. The prototype's pressed fill was
   about `#D7DAE0` in light, which the red words would read on at 3.9:1. Options for the owner: keep it so; let
   the plain secondary darken further than the destructive one; or darken the colored words (step 11), which moves
   every colored word in every theme (decisions/0012).
2. **watchOS heights** stay 32, 44 and 52 until the owner looks at the watch.
3. **Icon-only's scale** was the same in the prototype as beside a label; this implementation keeps that.
4. **The accessibility audit times out on one screen.** At accessibility5, the audit of the catalog's
   `button-states-destructive` page does not complete on its fifth screenful ("Audit failed to complete in time"),
   twice in a row, and it is not suppressed. With the icon at the medium scale it completes; with the destructive
   samples' glyph changed to `check` it still times out, and so it does with the spinner drawn unscaled. Every other
   test of the audit, and every other touched entry, passes. It is the same kind of failure as the known issue of
   #23 (decisions/0001, "Known issue on Apple"): no finding, an audit that does not finish on one screenful.
5. **A label on two lines.** At accessibility sizes a label that wraps makes the capsule taller, and its ends round
   with half that height; the snapshot matrix's accessibility5 sheet shows it, for the owner to judge.
