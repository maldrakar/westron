# 0002. The look of 2026-11-20

Date: 2026-11-20. Status: the owner's choice of the look, made on prototype renders, and the owner's acceptance of the
contrast deviation below; what the implementing agent decided on top of them is listed separately, pending the
owner's review with the snapshot matrix (decisions/0005, the second gate). It amends the approved contract, which stays
approved: the description, the option `lines`, the `tokens` block and `platforms` of `text-field.spec.yaml`, and the
proposals P4 and I4 of decisions/0001.

## Context

At the second gate of phase 3 the owner found the controls' function and colors right and their look not yet (#85).
After Button (its decisions/0003) the owner compared three prototype looks of the framed field and of the check's
unchecked ring, each beside the current ones, with Apple's own fields, on an iPhone 17 in light (catalog renders of a
throwaway prototype that changed no token, contract or WestronUI code; heights 28, 36 and 50 in all three):

- A, filled: `color.background.muted`, the secondary button's fill, no border, corners 10, 12 and 14.
- B, filled with an edge: the same fill and a `stroke.thin` edge in `color.border.default`, corners 8, 10 and 12.
- C, sunken: `color.background.sunken`, no border, corners 12, and a row with a capsule field beside a capsule button.

The public API stays: `.textFieldStyle(_:size:)` with the variants `framed` and `inline` and the sizes `s`, `m` and `l`,
on the system `TextField` and `SecureField`; the states, behavior and accessibility as the contract states them.

## The owner's decisions

1. **A framed field is filled, A's look.** `color.background.muted`, the same fill as the secondary button, and no
   border in the standard themes. Its text is unchanged: `body.regular` in `text.primary`.
2. **On one line it is a capsule**, the platform's capsule (`container.shape: capsule`), as C's bottom row drew it
   beside the capsule button, at the heights every control takes on iOS since 2026-11-20, 28, 36 and 50 (macOS 24,
   32 and 40).
3. **On several lines it cannot be a capsule**: a capsule's round ends would cut into the second line, so a field that
   grows with its text, the axis `vertical`, is a rounded rectangle. Its radius is the agent's (below).
4. **The contrast deviation is accepted in the standard themes.** The fill does not mark the field's boundary at 3:1
   against the ground, which WCAG 1.4.11 asks of a user interface component; the owner accepted it for the look, as
   Apple's own filled fields do. Measured with WCAG 2's formula on the tokens' 8-bit values (no declared pair holds
   the fill against a ground, so `tools/westron tokens contrast` does not list it):

   | Against | Light | Dark |
   |---|---|---|
   | `background.canvas` | 1.11 | 1.24 |
   | `background.surface` | 1.16 | 1.14 |
   | `background.sunken` | 1.04 | 1.30 |

   The outline it replaces, `border.strong`, measured 3.50, 3.66 and 3.29 in light and 4.12, 3.79 and 4.34 in dark
   (`tools/westron tokens contrast`).
   On the sunken ground in light the field is close to invisible; a product that puts a framed field there gives it
   another ground.
5. **The high-contrast themes keep the outline.** In `light-hc` and `dark-hc` a framed field also draws
   `color.border.strong` at `stroke.thin` around its fill, in every state, as the secondary button does there; the fill
   stays as quiet in those themes, which keep steps 1–5. `border.strong` meets 4.5:1 there against the grounds and the
   muted fill (5.27 and 6.22 against the canvas).
6. **Unchanged:** the `inline` variant, the focus ring (`color.border.focus` at `stroke.thick`, now following the
   field's shape, a capsule around a capsule), the text roles, `opacity.disabled`, the behavior, the public API and the
   accessibility labels.

| `container.paddingHorizontal`, framed | `space.100`, `space.150`, `space.200` | `space.200`, `space.250`, `space.300` |

P4 of decisions/0001 (the outline's width) now holds in high contrast only. I4's ring stays outside the field,
`space.25` away, and follows its shape; with no outline in the standard themes, focus still changes the field's shape,
from no line to one, so it is never shown by color alone.

## Decided by the implementing agent

Under the owner's instruction to implement the look, these are the agent's, for the owner to confirm at the review:

- **`lines`, a contract option that is the system's own axis.** The shape depends on whether the field has one line
  or grows, which no option of the contract said. It is `lines: [single, multiple]`, default `single`, given to the
  system's API on Apple as Button's `role` is (`systemOptions: { lines: SwiftUI.Axis }`): no enum is generated, the
  overload takes no argument, and a product writes the system's `TextField(_:text:axis: .vertical)` as before. On the
  web it is `<input>` and `<textarea>`, on GTK `GtkEntry` and `GtkTextView`. The snapshot matrix draws it as an axis,
  so it doubles to 36 combinations on 6 sheets. The alternative, a schema keyword for a condition that is not an option,
  was heavier and would have reached every generator.
- **The schema lets a `shape` bind in cases.** `container.shape` binds `capsule` for `framed` and `single` only, so a
  part's `shape` may now be a case binding whose tokens are shapes; a shape is still bound by nothing else
  (`spec/schema/component.schema.json`, `ContractRules.checkTokens`, `ContractCheckTests`).
- **The style reads the axis by reflection.** A `TextFieldStyle` is given the field itself, `TextField<_Label>`, and
  SwiftUI offers no public way to read its axis. The field stores it as `axis`, which `Mirror` finds: measured with
  Xcode 27.1 on macOS 26.7, iOS 26.3 and iOS 17.5 (an iPhone 15 simulator, `package-ios`) for
  `TextField(_:text:axis:)` with and without a prompt, a field with no axis, and `SecureField` (TextFieldStyleTests;
  the catalog's multiline cells show the rounded rectangle). A second test hosts styled fields in a window and reads
  what the style resolved from the configuration SwiftUI gave it (the package's `TextFieldResolvedAxes` preference):
  a horizontal field, a vertical one, a vertical one under `.lineLimit(1...4).padding()`, an inline one and a
  `SecureField`, so a SwiftUI that stops carrying the axis fails a test; it passes on macOS 26.7, iOS 26.3 and
  iOS 17.5. Where reflection
  finds no `axis`, as a future SwiftUI might store it otherwise, the style draws a single line, a capsule, and nothing
  else breaks. Rejected: deciding by the drawn height, which cannot tell an empty multiline field from a single one
  and would turn a field from a capsule into a rectangle as its text wraps; an argument of the style, an API change.
- **The radius of a multiline field: `radius.150`, 12**, the coordinator's assumption, from the existing radius
  ladder, the corner of prototype A at `m`. No new token: `radius.150` is a semantic step, as `space.200` is to Button.
  If the owner wants a role, `radius.field` aliasing it is the proposal.
- **Horizontal padding 16, 20 and 24** (`space.200`, `space.250`, `space.300`), Button's. Prototype C drew its capsule
  field 18 pt in at `m`, which is off the ladder; 16 and 20 are the steps either side. Button's padding makes a field
  and a button of one size, stacked in a form, start their text at the same distance from their round ends, and it
  keeps the text clear of the capsule's curve at every size. A multiline field takes the same padding, so a single and
  a multiline field of one size align their text.
- **Vertical padding on several lines: 4, 8 and 12** (`space.50`, `space.100`, `space.150`). Without it the lines
  touch the fill's top and bottom edges, an open point of decisions/0001 that the fill makes visible. The values put a
  multiline field of one line at the height of a single one: a line of body text is about 20.3 pt on iOS and 15.5 pt
  on macOS, so 4 + 20.3 + 4, 8 + 20.3 + 8 and 12 + 20.3 + 12 reach about 28, 36 and 44 on iOS, the last below the
  minimum 50, and 23.5, 31.5 and 39.5 on macOS, all within the minimum heights. A field of one line stays where it was;
  a field of several keeps its lines off the edge. Unlike the horizontal padding, it does not grow with the text size:
  it stands in for the centering a single line gets from the minimum height, which does not grow either. Grown, at
  accessibility5 a matrix cell holding one two-line multiline field at `l` was 255 pt tall, and the sheet's three rows
  of it did not fit an iPhone 17's screen; fixed, a single and a multiline field stay close in height at every text
  size. Even so, three rows of multiline cells holding an empty field and one of two lines do not fit one picture at
  accessibility5, and the matrix splits a sheet's columns there, not its rows; so on a matrix sheet at the
  accessibility sizes a multiline cell holds the two-line field alone (`TextFieldMatrixCell`). The `text-field` entry,
  which scrolls and which the accessibility audit walks, holds both at every text size.
- **A field keeps its height (after the review of #85).** At accessibility5 the card-notes sheet, which does not
  scroll, squeezed the card's one-line framed title below its text: the capsule about 36 pt, the text about 65. The
  style let it: offered 8 pt at accessibility5 on iOS 26.3, a framed field of one line took 36 pt for its 65, a framed
  multiline one 36 for 142, an inline multiline one 8 for 126; on macOS 26.7 the two multiline ones were squeezed
  likewise (TextFieldStyleTests, run before the change). A product layout short of height would do the same, so the
  style owns it: it fixes the field's vertical size, and the contract's description states that a field is never
  drawn lower than its text needs, in either variant. A multiline field still scrolls past the lines its text and the
  product's `lineLimit` give. The test proposes 8 pt at accessibility5 for both variants and both values of `lines`.
  So a product's fixed-height frame no longer squeezes a multiline field into scrolling: the field takes its text's
  height and spills past the frame. A product bounds a multiline field with `lineLimit`, not with a fixed-height frame;
  this is where westron's field differs from the system's.
- **The focus ring follows the shape.** Around a capsule it is a capsule `space.25` plus `stroke.thick` outside; around
  the rounded rectangle its corners grow by that distance, as before.
- **The high-contrast outline follows `colorSchemeContrast`**, as Button's does, and is drawn over the fill with
  `strokeBorder`, inside the field's edge.

## Open

1. **The placeholder on the fill.** The system's placeholder color, which a `TextFieldStyle` cannot change
   (decisions/0001, I1), now stands on the muted fill instead of the canvas; its contrast was measured on the canvas
   only (1.72:1 on iOS in light). It stays the open point I1 was.
2. **A field on the sunken ground** reads at 1.04:1 in light; whether a product needs a framed field there, and what
   it would do, is left to the first consumer.
3. **watchOS** draws the system field unchanged, as before.
4. **Initial focus on macOS.** Once the style fixed a field's height, the macOS matrix's card-notes sheet opened with
   the first card's title holding the keyboard focus for real, its text selected, in every theme; with the fixed size
   taken out for a trial, it did not, and the generated sheets, whose first field is an empty one, never did. Why the
   window picks that field is not understood. A window that focuses its first text field is the platform's behavior,
   so the style does not fight it; the catalog's sample takes the focus away, so the rest row shows rest.
